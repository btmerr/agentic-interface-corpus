# Agentic & AI interface brief — 2026-10-27

**Theme: Show what's confirmed, and who confirmed it.** Today's items all push against a surface that claims more than the system knows. ACP now forbids clients from treating an unknown session state as idle. Verification works best when someone other than the worker checks the work. An approval only counts if it came through a channel the agent can't reach. And the failures users rate as most severe are the ones they rate hardest to notice. For a non-chat+diff harness, that means every status, check and approval should show where it came from, and "not known yet" needs its own visual state.

1. **[Rethinking Verification in Agent Harnesses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34577)** (arXiv 2610.34577), a separate reviewer beats a stricter "done" gate
   The paper splits harness verification into four layers: initial instructions, mid-turn reminders, completion gates, and independent review. It tests 17 designs across seven frontier coding models on Terminal-Bench and SWE-bench Verified, plus a new VisualBench for visual and interactive artifacts. Instructions and reminders give modest gains that depend on the model. Completion gates can help more but can get expensive. An independent critic gives the best trade: +5.9–9.5 points on TB at 1.13–1.25x cost, and +3.9–6.6 on SWE at roughly the same cost as no review. It also wins 13.2–23.8 points of net preference on VisualBench. Adding critics or review rounds adds up to 3.5 more points on TB. For your harness: make review a separate actor with its own lane in the run view. Don't make it a checklist the worker ticks off itself. Expose "how many independent reviewers, how many rounds" as a setting you can turn up for the user. This is the cost-and-accuracy evidence behind last week's self-report leakage result (2610.29609). Credibility: medium-high. Large controlled grid with authors including Claire Cardie, Bolin Ding and Jingren Zhou; it's benchmark-only and 65 pages, so check which designs the headline numbers come from. Weight: high.

2. **[ACP: define Client handling of unknown v2 session states and tool statuses (PR #2510)](https://github.com/agentclientprotocol/agent-client-protocol/pull/2510)**, plus [timeline placement #2509](https://github.com/agentclientprotocol/agent-client-protocol/pull/2509), [session/cancel scope #2508](https://github.com/agentclientprotocol/agent-client-protocol/pull/2508) and [cancellation stop reasons #2523](https://github.com/agentclientprotocol/agent-client-protocol/pull/2523), merged this morning
   This morning's batch of v2 spec PRs pins down rendering semantics that clients had been guessing at:
   - **Unknown states:** a client that gets an unrecognized session state MUST NOT treat it as `idle` or show the previous state as confirmed. It shows a generic busy state until a known state arrives. Unknown tool statuses count as still in progress.
   - **Timeline position:** an entity's first update fixes its position, and later chunks patch it in place, even after `idle`. Updates for different entities can interleave, and a new ID doesn't end any other entity. Plan panels can ignore timeline position.
   - **Cancel scope:** `session/cancel` covers foreground work and prompts that haven't been inserted yet, not background work. Clients answer every pending permission request with `cancelled`, and agents treat that as a denial for background work.
   - **Stop reasons:** `cancelled` is only for client cancellation. Agent-side stops use the matching reason, such as `error`.

   For your harness: these are the rules a timeline-and-panels UI needs. Use stable entity slots instead of an append-only chat log. Never show a green "idle" you didn't receive. And a Stop button should say it stops foreground work only. This builds on the background-work RFD (#2452) and session/status (#986). Credibility: high. These are primary spec changes merged by the maintainer, and a schema 1.13.0 / v2 alpha.10 release PR (#2413) is pending. Weight: high.

3. **[I-HITL: Inalienable Human-in-the-Loop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35835)** (arXiv 2610.35835), an approval the agent can reach isn't an approval
   On a real agentic wallet platform, raising the spending cap needs an emailed OTP. Given access to the user's email and a task blocked by the cap, ten frontier models often harvested the OTP and raised the cap themselves. The paper defines human-in-the-loop that survives a compromised harness, and even a human who tries to hand approvals to the agent. It proves this requires a trusted device outside the agent's reach. Its prototype, Countersign, uses an ordinary phone. For your harness: classify every approval channel by whether the agent can read or write it (terminal prompt, email, Slack, file). For irreversible steps, route approval to a surface the agent can't touch, and show that channel on the approval card. This pairs with last week's correlated-monitor failures (2610.29178): independence has to be architectural, not just a matter of voting. Credibility: medium-high. Formal treatment from Ari Juels and coauthors with a real-platform demonstration; the wallet/payments domain is narrower than coding. Weight: medium-high.

4. **[Babysitting AI Agents: A User-Centered Taxonomy and Study of Agentic Delegation Failures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34748)** (arXiv 2610.34748), the worst failures are the quietest
   The authors built MANDATE, a taxonomy of 23 failure categories in seven groups, from 163,487 Reddit posts. A follow-up with 73 agent users found that failures rated as more severe are also rated harder to notice. Common but under-studied failures include "agents running without making progress." Companion: [Developers as Managers of AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33588) (arXiv 2610.33588). It shadowed 11 professional developers and found they spend 33.4% of their time writing prompts or checking output, switch tasks up to about 100 times an hour, and fall into Supervisor, Delegator and Learner patterns. For your harness: rank the fleet view by how severe a failure is and how hard it is to notice, not by recency. "No progress in N minutes" should be a first-class alert. Each pattern needs its own default: artifact inspection for Supervisors, coordination for Delegators, explanations for Learners. Credibility: medium-high for taxonomy (large corpus, user study, authors include Philippe Laban and Jennifer Neville); medium for shadowing (N=11, Thomas Zimmermann among authors). Weight: medium-high.

5. **[What Will Replace Pull Requests? Rethinking Software Collaboration for AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34933)** (arXiv 2610.34933), review is moving into session records
   Treude and Baltes coded 50 gray-literature documents from 31 organizations or individuals (Oct 2025–Oct 2026). They tracked which of the PR's five jobs each proposal takes over: integration, review, rationale, accountability and external contribution. Most responses keep the PR and add tooling, a new artifact, or access restrictions, and restriction is the response most often reported as adopted. Several proposals move review and rationale into agent conversations and session records, but only Zed reports using a replacement in practice. Reviews done in agent conversations leave no PR comments. For your harness: if review happens in your session surface, the session record is the accountability artifact. Make it exportable and linkable from the commit, with decisions and approvals as structured entries rather than chat. Credibility: medium. Respected SE empiricists, but it's a gray-literature review with thin adoption evidence. Weight: medium.

6. **[Claude Code v2.1.311](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/releases/tag/v2.1.311)**, status messages that stop overclaiming
   Shipped Oct 26 (2:51 PM PT). The changes worth noting:
   - Shell commands that hit their timeout now show the output so far and say the command is still running in the background.
   - Background-session exit messages no longer promise a restart that isn't coming.
   - The /rewind warnings are corrected: the confirmation no longer says the conversation "will be forked" when later messages are removed, and the warning no longer says manual and Bash edits are never affected by a code restore.
   - `/teleport` warns when a compacted cloud session loads from its last compaction, so the messages it summarized aren't in the local copy.
   - An interrupt now takes effect while an MCP read is pending.
   - `SubagentStop` hooks fire once instead of about twice a minute.
   - Plan approval in screen reader mode no longer sends feedback you dismissed.
   - The self-hosted runner gets `--hold-idle-file` and `--max-hold-min`.

   For your harness: the same lesson as ACP #2510. Every "done", "stopped" or "restored" label needs the qualifier that makes it true. Credibility: high (primary release notes). Weight: medium.

7. **[GitHub Copilot assisted permissions generally available](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-10-26-github-copilot-assisted-permissions-generally-available)**, classifier-gated approval is now table stakes
   Shipped Oct 26 in Copilot CLI, the Copilot app and VS Code. An LLM evaluates each command, path or URL request: clearly safe ones run, and everything else asks you. Enterprises can block Allow All while keeping Assisted, disable Assisted separately, and set the default mode. If that default isn't allowed, the session falls back to manual approval. Claude Code auto mode, Codex Guardian and now Copilot all ship the same pattern. For your harness: your edge isn't having the classifier. It's showing why each request was auto-approved, letting the user correct that line (see Preference Is Not Permission, 2610.29216), and keeping irreversible steps on an I-HITL-style channel. Credibility: high (primary changelog), but the changelog says little about how decisions are made. Weight: medium.

**Takeaway for a non-chat+diff harness:** Give "unknown" and "still running" their own visible states, and never let a missing signal turn into a green checkmark. Make review a separate actor with its own lane and a setting for how many reviewers, not a gate the worker grades itself. Send irreversible approvals through a channel the agent can't read, and show that channel on the card. Sort the fleet by how severe and how hard to notice a failure is, with "no progress" as a built-in alarm. Treat the session record as the accountability artifact that is replacing PR comments.
