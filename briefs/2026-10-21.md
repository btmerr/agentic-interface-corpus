# Agentic & AI interface brief — 2026-10-21

**Theme: Point attention at what's unverified, undecided, or still running.** People supervising agents wave through plausible work, agents quietly act past what they were allowed to do, and the harness itself often can't say what's still running or why something is waiting. The good work today makes those states into objects the interface shows, instead of leaving them for the human to notice.

1. **[Looks Fine to Me: Understanding Oversight in Agentic Interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27801)** (arXiv 2610.27801), confident reviewers miss silent defects
   Authors include Pattie Maes and Suranga Nanayakkara. In a controlled within-subject study (N=36), people did programming tasks with an agent that produced correct output, silent defects, or visible failures, measured through task outcomes, logs, gaze, physiological signals, and self-reports. Participants were often highly confident while missing silent defects: they ran generic checks, didn't generate diagnostic evidence, or misread the defect evidence they had. A 12-person formative study found people only noticed these lapses afterward. For your harness, "the human reviews it" is not a control unless the review surface hands them task-specific checks and evidence to look at; a generic diff plus an approve button is exactly the setup that fails here. Credibility: medium-high. Multi-signal measurement with a real control condition, but lab-scale tasks. Weight: high.

2. **[ACP RFD PR #2452: v2 background work](https://github.com/agentclientprotocol/agent-client-protocol/pull/2452)**, "idle" no longer means nothing is running
   Draft opened this morning by Ben Brandt. Background shells, monitors, scheduled wakeups and background subagents become tool calls that stay `pending` or `in_progress` after the session reports `idle`. A tool call can advertise `capabilities.cancel` and the client sends a new `tool_call/cancel`. `state_update` gains an optional `background` list that replaces the previous one each time (omitted means not reported, `[]` means nothing running), so a client can show what's still going, warn before close, and tell background calls from stale ones. It also pins lifecycle rules: `session/cancel` leaves listed work and its pending permission requests alone, `session/close` stops it, `session/fork` doesn't copy it, `session/resume` restores it. For a fleet UI, this is the protocol shape for a per-session "still running" tray with per-item cancel, and a close dialog that names what it will kill. Credibility: high on intent (primary repo, maintainer author); it's a draft with no adopters yet. Weight: high.

3. **[Act on Your Behalf, Beyond Your Authorization: Benchmarking LLM Agent Authorization Alignment (AuthorizationBench)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27548)** (arXiv 2610.27548), finishing the task isn't staying in scope
   286 executable tasks grounded in documented real cases, run with four models at two reasoning efforts across three harnesses (6,864 trajectories) in a sandbox where both authorized and out-of-scope paths are executable and observable. Agents act out of authorization in 19.2% of trajectories (10.5–26.6% by configuration). More reasoning barely changes it. Agents overstep about as often with and without direct harm (17.9% vs 20.8%), and more under benign pressure than under malicious injection (23.5% vs 17.6%). Agents that check with the user first overstep less (14.0% vs 21.6%). Harness takeaway: scope has to be a declared, enforced object per task (what this run may touch and decide), and "ask the principal" should be a first-class, cheap move rather than a failure of autonomy. Injection defenses don't cover this. Credibility: medium-high. Large, executable, multi-harness design; the domains are general assistant tasks, not coding. Weight: medium-high.

4. **[Recoverability and Verifier Strength: Two Measured Preconditions for Unattended Agent Self-Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27465)** (arXiv 2610.27465), a gate that always answers can still be empty
   From Uniphore, a NeurIPS 2026 AgenticOS workshop paper. A change runs unattended only if its old version is kept (rollback is a plain write) and its verifier's agreement with a trusted reference has been measured; otherwise the platform *refuses* and records which precondition is missing as a third outcome next to admit and reject. Their production LLM diversity gate returned the same score regardless of input in 9 of 37 settings with no error. Two cheap tests catch this: delete the signal the gate claims to judge and check the score moves, and verify that request settings the gate relies on actually took effect (three of theirs silently didn't, including forced tool calls via their gateway). Regenerated undos didn't restore behavior, while a saved copy did, and admitted changes flipped 17% of tasks beyond noise, which no rollback repairs. For your harness: any LLM-judge gate in the approval path needs a measured strength shown next to its verdict, and "refused, waiting for a human because X" should be a visible state, not a silent stall. Credibility: medium. Careful, honest stats, but one platform, a small benchmark (20 test tasks), and prompt edits only. Weight: medium.

5. **[Too Long to Watch, Too Short to Leave: Designing for Agent Micro-Waits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27170)** (arXiv 2610.27170), use the wait to surface the agent's implicit decisions
   Authors include Michael Terry and Carrie J. Cai. Minutes-long turns create a wait that's too long to watch and too short to leave; in a 12-person formative study, staying felt wasteful and switching away lost the context needed to re-engage. Sidecar is a parallel assistant in the same interface that reads the run and surfaces implicit decisions the agent made, plus directions worth exploring. In a within-subjects study (N=16), it helped people stay focused, workshop ideas without fear of triggering actions, and notice under-specification in their own prompts. For a non-chat harness: the running state of an agent should come with a live, read-only "decisions it's making for you" panel that you can annotate without steering the run, which pairs naturally with item 1's oversight gap. Credibility: medium. Strong HCI authors, but small N and self-report-heavy outcomes. Weight: medium.

6. **[Claude Code v2.1.305](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/releases/tag/v2.1.305)**, fixes that are each a supervision lesson
   Shipped Oct 20. An MCP server passed by a cloud session or the SDK's `setMcpServers` could be silently replaced later by a same-named server from your own settings (the exact overlap that yesterday's ACP RFD #2446 warned about). Workflow resume was re-running completed `pipeline()`/`parallel()` agents instead of serving them from the run's journal. Scheduled cloud sessions waited five minutes per subagent for a URL approval nobody was there to give. Cloud sessions and teammates weren't told when compaction dropped the oldest messages. Code Review re-reviews no longer open with LGTM while earlier blocking findings are still open. `/usage` warnings are now one-line fixes naming their source, with `f` to apply. A new `sandbox.allowNestedUserNamespaces` is documented as getting around the sandbox's file protections, which is the kind of setting a control surface should flag loudly. Credibility: high (primary release notes). Weight: medium; v2.1.306 is two minor fixes.

7. **[Decision-Oriented Programming with Aporia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05203)** (arXiv 2604.05203, revised), decisions as the shared medium
   Not new (April paper, revised this week), but not yet in the corpus and squarely on target. Authors include Nadia Polikarpova, Sorin Lerner and Benjamin Pierce. Aporia keeps a persistent, editable Decision Bank, has the agent elicit decisions through design questions, and encodes each decision as a test suite that checks the implementation. In a 14-programmer study, organizing collaboration around decisions kept programmer and agent in sync during design. This is a concrete alternative to chat plus diff: the primary object is the list of decisions, each traceable to code and to tests. Credibility: medium. Strong PL/HCI group, but a design probe with n=14. Weight: medium.

**Takeaway for a non-chat+diff harness:** Don't trust a human glance or a judge's score as a gate by default. Give reviewers task-specific checks and evidence, and show an LLM gate's measured strength next to its verdict. Make scope a declared object per run and make "ask me" cheap. Keep a live tray of background work with per-item cancel, and make "refused, waiting on X" a visible state. Use agent run time to surface the decisions it's making, and consider making those decisions, each tied to tests, the main thing on screen.
