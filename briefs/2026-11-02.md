# Agentic & AI interface brief — 2026-11-02

**Theme: Show the human what's actually in play at the moment they decide.** Today's papers find that approvers judging a command's text miss what it does, that good after-the-fact diagnosers make poor live supervisors, and that when several agents share one person, the order of their questions matters as much as the questions. ACP gave background work and paused tool calls a real place in the protocol. The harness job is to put effects, live state and pending asks in front of the person, not just text and transcripts.

1. **["Shall We Approve?" Consequence-Aware Approval for Autonomous Coding Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40753)** (arXiv 2610.40753), approve what a command did, not what it says
   Caper runs each shell command first in a snapshotted sandbox, hands an independent approver a bounded report of the observed effects, commits allowed commands and rolls back blocked ones with a reason the agent can act on. Against six existing approvers (rule checkers, guardrail models, deployed permission classifiers), it had the lowest harmful-outcome rate on SABER (6.7% vs at least 14.8%) and on YoloFS-Ext, a new benchmark where the harm hides inside invoked tools (7.8% vs at least 35.3%), while blocking only 1.0% of the safe twins. For your harness: the approval card should show the effects (files touched, network, processes) from a dry run, not just the command string, and a rejection should roll back and return a reason the agent can use. Credibility: medium (six authors, two benchmarks, one of them their own). Weight: high. Complements Oct 29's CASEL (2610.36570) by putting the consequence report in front of the approver.

2. **[ReplayRCA: Measuring Post-hoc Diagnosis and Live Oversight of Agent Failures by Counterfactual Replay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41025)** (arXiv 2610.41025), the best autopsy model isn't the best live supervisor
   It scores a diagnosis by intervention: restore the state before the chosen step, swap in the proposed action, let the same agent continue, and subtract a placebo replay. On 62 calibrated failures, the best of 13 models repaired +51.3 points, and the top eight were statistically tied. Forcing the fix onto the last step cost 17.1 points. In live oversight (one intervention allowed, before an irreversible action, not knowing whether the run will fail), a small open-weight model tied for best, while one of the strongest post-hoc diagnosers was the weakest live supervisor (-14.5 points, p = 0.002). For your harness: a supervisor role and a post-mortem role are different jobs and should be benchmarked and picked separately. "Rewind to step N and try this instead" is a powerful control, and it should be measured against simply replaying the original step. Credibility: medium (two authors and a synthetic back-office world, but placebo-controlled with code released). Weight: high.

3. **[When Should AI Agents Ask? Clarification Requests Under Limited Human Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41129)** (arXiv 2610.41129), a fleet's questions need one shared queue
   It models several agents sharing one person: each can search, ask, or keep doing work that doesn't depend on the missing answer. Whether to ask depends on more than the agent's own uncertainty. An agent with other work left should often ask earlier, and the best order for answering depends on every waiting question, so no per-agent ranking is optimal. Their pairwise policy came within 0.07% of optimal on 864 synthetic cases and cut cost 18-31% versus three standard rules for when to ask. For your harness: route clarification requests from all agents into one inbox ordered by how much each answer unblocks, and let an agent keep working on independent parts while its question waits. Credibility: low-medium (a stylized model with proofs; the LLM case study is small). Weight: medium-high for fleet design.

4. **[ACP: add background work to v2 (PR #2738)](https://github.com/agentclientprotocol/agent-client-protocol/pull/2738)**, plus [#2745](https://github.com/agentclientprotocol/agent-client-protocol/pull/2745) and [#2736 session archive](https://github.com/agentclientprotocol/agent-client-protocol/pull/2736), background work gets a real home in the protocol
   Merged Oct 30 (about 2:49 PM PT), draft v2 only. Tool calls get a `paused` status, and `pending` now means "hasn't started yet". Each tool call or terminal can advertise whether it can be cancelled, with new per-item `tool_call/cancel` and `terminal/cancel` methods. Running, idle and requires-action snapshots can carry a `background` list of terminals, tool calls and subagents still going, and a malformed list counts as "not reported", not as empty. #2745 (this morning, about 5:38 AM PT) admits agents often can't know a follow-up turn is coming when background work ends, so clients should wait a short grace period instead of flashing idle. #2736 adds archive and unarchive, with an archived session reading as `saved`. For your harness: "idle" should still show what's running in the background, cancel should be offered per item only where it's supported, and "unknown" must look different from "nothing". Credibility: high (primary spec). Weight: medium-high. Implements the background-work RFD from Oct 21 ([#2452](https://github.com/agentclientprotocol/agent-client-protocol/pull/2452)).

5. **[Evidence-Guided Recovery for AI Agents with Ambiguous Tool Outcomes and Partial Execution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41453)** (arXiv 2610.41453), plus [Cross-Interface Authorization for AI Computer-Use Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41397) (2610.41397), recovering after "may have run"
   After a timeout, an action may have failed, committed or still be in flight. The first paper plans recovery against the evidence available and separates "goal actually satisfied" from "safely completed as far as the evidence shows". Planning advice certified 30 completions versus 9 for plain evidence prompting across 58 paired units, and unknown or handoff outcomes stay explicit. The companion paper, from the same authors, checks authorization again at commit time on whichever route (GUI, CLI, API, MCP) carries the effect, and only counts something as committed with a receipt from the system that received it. For your harness: after an "unknown outcome" state, offer a recovery step grounded in evidence ("check whether the charge exists") rather than a blind retry, and only show "done" with a receipt. Credibility: low-medium (small finite studies with local models; the second is mostly formal results bound to one declared deployment). Weight: medium. Builds on Oct 30's honest tool-call states.

6. **[Claude Code v2.1.316](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/releases/tag/v2.1.316)**, fewer things happening where you can't see them
   Released Oct 30 (about 6:01 PM PT):
   - Cloud sessions now show a form card when an MCP server asks for input, instead of cancelling the request without you seeing it.
   - A "Catching up…" line shows while a session you switch back to is still syncing its messages.
   - The Changes pane's note on what the list leaves out is now a badge beside the branch name.
   - Shell commands are no longer left running when Claude Code exits by itself.
   - Cloud sessions and scheduled tasks are now marked failed when auto mode stopped a turn for lack of a safety verdict, and `claude -p` says why auto mode stopped.

   For your harness: any request the agent or a tool makes of the user must surface somewhere visible, even when nobody is watching that session. Show "still syncing" as its own state, and never let a stopped run look like a success. Credibility: high (primary release notes). Weight: medium.

7. **[Inkitiate: Initiating and Steering Agent Assistance through Ink in a Spatial Workspace](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39905)** (arXiv 2610.39905), a non-chat way to direct an agent
   On a spatial canvas, people use handwriting and sketches to pull in materials, build on earlier results, reorganize the space, and inspect and guide agent actions. Eighteen participants reported good usability and low workload, and reused leftover ink and materials as new queries. The paper catalogs how people mix words and drawn marks (circling, arrows, grouping) to direct the agent. For your harness: pointing at and grouping things on a persistent surface, such as files, plan steps or diffs, can be the request itself, and marks that stay on the canvas become reusable context. Credibility: low-medium (a knowledge-work tool, not coding, with small studies). Weight: medium as interface inspiration.

**Takeaway for a non-chat+diff harness:** Approve on observed effects from a dry run. Pick live supervisors separately from post-mortem models. Run one prioritized question queue across the fleet. Keep background work, "unknown" outcomes and pending tool requests visible until each one actually resolves.
