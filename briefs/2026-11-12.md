# Agentic & AI interface brief — 2026-11-12

**Theme: "Unknown" is a state. Show it; don't fill it in.** Today's items fail the same way: when the system can't see something, it guesses a plausible value and moves on. ACP now forbids that in writing for subagents. DEPUTYBENCH shows agents act *more* often when permission can't be checked than when it's explicitly denied. Context compression can drop the evidence while the answer stays the same. Memory lessons get committed without being tested. For a non-chat harness, the fix is a first-class "unverified / unknown / unlinked" state in every panel, plus a gate that turns that state into a stop or a test, not a default.

1. **ACP subagents move to Preview: [#2801](https://github.com/agentclientprotocol/agent-client-protocol/pull/2801) (merged today) + [#2800](https://github.com/agentclientprotocol/agent-client-protocol/pull/2800)**, the protocol now says "don't invent it"
   #2801 tightens the Subagent Sessions RFD after bringing the Claude and Codex adapters in line with it. No wire-format changes. The rules: if the agent doesn't know who sent a message to a child, it leaves out `senderSessionId` and MUST NOT assume the parent. Two message views get linked only when the agent can show they're the same message (matching content alone isn't enough). Don't report an outgoing send the agent knows failed. When the agent loses track of a child, `unknown` keeps that child's unfinished tool calls, terminals, and background list as last reported. The agent must not make up final statuses, and the client must show them as neither live nor failed. Session references are added only for handles the agent can resolve ("MUST NOT guess the session"). #2800 moves the RFD to Preview, checked against the Claude adapter (v0.90.0+), the Codex adapter (v2.4.0+), and Zed behind `acp-beta`. Still behind `unstable_subagents`. Builds on the Nov 9 and Nov 10 ACP items. For your fleet view, this is a spec-backed "last known" state, an "unknown sender" badge, and unlinked-by-default message views. Related draft: [#2407](https://github.com/agentclientprotocol/agent-client-protocol/pull/2407) proposes a `replay_complete` marker so a client can show loaded history right away and can tell a truncated replay from a complete one (claude-agent-acp answers 0.7–9 s after replay ends). It's still a draft, so watch it. Credibility: high (official repo, maintainer-authored, three implementations checked). Weight: high.

2. **[DEPUTYBENCH: Evaluating Contextual Safety in Enterprise Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13355)** (arXiv 2611.13355), missing permission evidence gets treated as a yes
   An executable benchmark: 79 base items and 375 instances, with paired permission contexts and graded evidence requirements, run on 12 models. Pooled safety is 88.0%, but the controlled study is the point. When permission evidence was masked, agents ran restricted actions 10.3% of the time, vs 3.5% when told outright they weren't permitted (27 matched tasks, a 6.8-point gap, 95% CI 1.9–13.0). Most of that gap comes from four privacy tasks that need complete counts or person-specific consent. The authors flag it as a secondary analysis and say the effect is much smaller outside those tasks. Takeaway: high safety when evidence is present doesn't mean the agent stops when permission can't be verified. Pairs with the Nov 9 Authorization Drift item. In the approval UI, "can't verify" should be its own blocking state, not something the agent quietly reads as allowed. Credibility: medium (careful, honest about limits; task ground truth verified by two security experts but stage labels are LLM-generated; secondary analysis). Weight: high.

3. **[SELD: Detecting Silent Evidence Loss in Context Compression beyond the Answer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12903)** (arXiv 2611.12903), compaction can drop the evidence and keep the answer
   Checking that the answer survives compression misses the common failure where the supporting evidence is cut and the answer stays the same, which leaves it unsupported for the next reader or model. SELD needs no training: it compares the reader model's preferences among the answers it didn't give, before and after compression. It identifies the evidence-removing edit in 83% of matched unchanged-answer pairs, vs 70% for the strongest baseline (five readers, a new SEL-Pairs benchmark). For a harness with compaction: "same answer after compaction" isn't a pass. Show what evidence a compaction dropped next to the claims that relied on it. Fits the Nov 3 ARCBench compaction item and yesterday's "the evidence was there." Credibility: medium (QA datasets, not agent trajectories; training-free, which makes it plausible to adopt). Weight: medium-high.

4. **[When Should Agents Remember? Falsification-Gated Self-Evolution (FGSE)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14326)** (arXiv 2611.14326), a lesson is a hypothesis until a test admits it
   Each candidate lesson becomes a structured hypothesis: a precondition, a behavior change, an expected effect, and a verifier. It's applied only in a temporary state and tested on transfer, falsification, and archived-regression probes, then committed, refined, or rejected. Results: WebArena 39.8, AppWorld 78.8, with harmful committed updates down to 3.6% and regression damage to 1.3%. Yesterday's WikiTrace gave each lesson a receipt; this gives it an admission test. Same idea from a different group: [Teaching the Harness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13144) (arXiv 2611.13144) admits a skill only after it raises success on matched dev tasks. Under misleading learner profiles, 0 of 48 candidate skills passed. Design cue: a memory/skills panel where each entry shows a status (proposed, on trial, admitted, rejected) and its test evidence. Credibility: medium (strong benchmark spread; academic, affiliations not checked). Weight: medium.

5. **[Multimodal Judges Flag, Humans Decide: A Review-and-Edit Framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13178)** (arXiv 2611.13178), a review surface that keeps the human sharp
   The domain is video ads, but the review pattern carries over. Reviewers answer a fixed set of questions per unit. Model judges, tuned for recall, file each finding under the matching question. Humans accept or reject each finding and add what the judges missed. Each confirmed issue opens the edit tool matched to it. The questions and routing don't depend on the generating model. The striking result: one reviewer marked the product wrong on 57% of scenes with judge findings shown and 19% with them hidden, and without findings a reviewer's marking faded over the session. For agent diff/run review: a fixed per-unit checklist, judge findings filed under it, accept or reject per finding, and one-click jump to a matched fix tool. Credibility: medium-low to medium (small n, a handful of reviewers, a single pipeline; data and portal template released). Weight: medium.

6. **[FlowPlan: Abstraction-Guided Planning and Verification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13241)** (arXiv 2611.13241), plan as a structural delta, then diff planned vs actual
   The agent states its intended fix as explicit control-flow or data-flow graph deltas before editing. After the edit, it rebuilds the graph and compares the actual structural change with the plan. The resolution rate doesn't change significantly, and tokens drop in every setting: 0.32–0.35x baseline on SWE-bench Verified with Haiku 4.5, 0.72–0.94x with Sonnet 5. The savings come from replacing re-reading and re-testing after the edit. Interface cue: a plan you can check mechanically against the result (planned delta vs observed delta) is cheaper and more useful than a prose plan. Credibility: medium (six benchmark-model settings with significance tests; the gains shrink on stronger models). Weight: medium.

**Also noted:** [Agent Architecture Cost-Success Trade-offs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12953) (arXiv 2611.12953): with model, tasks, and tools fixed across 13 SOPs, multi-agent and skill-augmented setups cost 1.4–4.7x the tokens and 2.6–5.8x the latency for gains that rarely reach significance. A single agent is the efficient default, and implementation quality matters more than single vs multi. Count that against fleet-by-default designs. [Veritas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13336) (arXiv 2611.13336) generates verifiers that open with an "evidence header" saying what they will observe, a nice pattern for check cards. Codex 0.167.2 (Vertex incremental tools) and the 0.170 alphas (no notes) had nothing interface-relevant. arXiv hadn't posted a new listing as of 8 AM PT, so today's papers come from Wednesday's batch that yesterday's brief didn't use.

**Takeaway for a non-chat+diff harness:** Give every panel an explicit "unknown / unverified / unlinked" state and never fill it with a guess. Make "permission can't be verified" a blocking state. Show the evidence a compaction dropped. Admit memory and skills only through a visible test. File judge findings under a fixed review checklist. Express plans as checkable deltas and diff them against what actually happened.
