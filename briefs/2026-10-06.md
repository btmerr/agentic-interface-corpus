# Agentic & AI interface brief — 2026-10-06

**Theme:** Supervision is moving off the transcript. Approvals are becoming a swappable reviewer with its own state machine and circuit breaker, sessions are becoming first-class editor objects instead of chats, and "what did the agent do" is becoming a phase-aware graph or a set of lanes instead of scrollback.

1. **[Codex Auto-review (formerly Guardian)](https://developers.openai.com/codex/concepts/sandboxing/auto-review)**: swap who approves, never what's allowed
   When the agent asks to cross the sandbox boundary, the request goes to a separate reviewer agent instead of you. It can't widen writable roots, network, or protected paths. The reviewer sees a compact transcript plus the exact request, but not hidden reasoning. A denial comes back with a rationale and an instruction to find a materially safer path or stop and ask. A per-turn circuit breaker interrupts after 3 denials in a row or 10 in the last 50, timeouts are reported separately from denials, and `/approve` opens a picker that lets you grant one retry of one exact denied action, which is still re-reviewed. CLI 0.160.0 (Oct 1) added opt-in history retrieval and handoff context for reviews. This is the most complete spec I've seen for an agent-as-approver lane, and the breaker plus the narrow one-shot override are directly stealable. Credibility: high. It's OpenAI's official docs with an open-source policy, and they say plainly it isn't a deterministic security guarantee. Weight: high.

2. **[JetBrains Air in IDEs (EAP)](https://blog.jetbrains.com/ai/2026/10/air-in-ides-eap/)**: the session, not the chat, is the unit of work
   JetBrains says outright that running agents in parallel is fundamentally different from chatting, and they rebuilt around it. There's a cross-project session list showing activity, unread updates, changed files, outgoing commits, and per-session cost. Sessions open as editor tabs, and you choose per session between a TUI, a terminal, or a graphical chat. Temporary worktrees let you cherry-pick results back, and any ACP agent (Codex, Copilot, Claude Agent, Junie, Cursor) plugs in. It's a major IDE vendor validating "board, not scroll", and the per-session choice of renderer is worth copying. Credibility: high, since it's the official launch post, but it's an EAP with no usage data, and cloud runs need a JetBrains AI subscription. Weight: medium-high.

3. **[Graphectory Viewer (arXiv 2608.17195, ASE '26 tool)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17195)**: trajectories as phase-aware graphs
   It turns raw runs into graphs colored by phase (localization, patch, validation). The labels depend on context, so `pytest` before an edit counts as localization and after an edit as validation. Repeated actions collapse into shared nodes, which makes loops and backtracking visible, and every node links back to its full thought, action, and observation. There's also a file-footprint strip and a Sankey diagram of phase transitions across many runs. Trajectories averaging about 35,700 lines compress to roughly 35 nodes. Credibility: medium. It's a UIUC tool paper with open code, a demo, and a corpus of about 4,000 trajectories, but the user study was 5 people (8% vs 84% task accuracy against the SWE-agent viewer). Weight: medium-high.

4. **[Agent ATO (arXiv 2609.08301)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08301)**: four lanes and a token split
   It's a cheaper cousin of Graphectory. Each interaction gets one mark, with filtered lanes for Discovery, Reading, Writing, and Execution that keep everything else in gray for context, plus a token graph aligned to the same sequence that separates the model's own output from tool results. In its case studies it caught edits never followed by a test run, and it told "the agent is overthinking" apart from "the agent read a huge file". That would work as a live strip on every session card. Credibility: medium-low. It's a short University of Osaka paper with one agent, two tasks, unvalidated rule-based tags, and no user study. Weight: medium.

5. **[Session Control (Agent Surface guide)](https://agentsurface.dev/docs/agentic-ui/session-control)**: approvals as a persisted state machine
   It's a tight checklist for the layer between the UI and the agent loop. Modes are visible, switchable policies you can downgrade mid-run, and subagents collapse into labeled units you can expand. Approvals have explicit states (drafted, needs_approval, approved, rejected, expired, executed, rolled_back), and each record captures who approved, what they saw at that moment, what changed afterward, and which execution consumed the approval. It also names failure modes worth testing against, such as ephemeral "always allow", steering that forces a restart, and auto-accept with no way back to draft. Credibility: medium. It's an independent guide with no named author and no new evidence, but it's unusually precise. Weight: medium.

6. **[Agency-Aware Interface Patterns (ICMI '26 Companion)](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3776591.3832499)**: a coverage audit
   It defines six pattern families mapped to five functions of delegated agency: granting, bounding, inspecting, intervening, and withdrawing (stop, revoke, roll back, off-switch). It's handy for checking what your harness is missing, and most are thin on withdrawal and on grounding results in evidence. Credibility: medium. It's peer-reviewed companion work published Oct 5 that the authors call an initial catalogue, and I could only read the abstract and excerpts. Weight: low-medium.

**Takeaway for a non-chat+diff harness:** Make the approver a slot that a human or a reviewer agent can fill, give it a persisted state machine, a denial circuit breaker, and a narrow one-shot override, and log what the approver saw. Make the session card the primary object, with a choice of renderer per session, and put a phase or lane strip on it so a glance tells you whether the agent edited without testing or is stuck in a loop.
